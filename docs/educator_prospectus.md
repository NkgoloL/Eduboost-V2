# EduBoost: An Educator Prospectus

**Discussion copy | 23 November 2026**

This prospectus is for educators who want to understand EduBoost and its Longitudinal Educational Validation (LEV) workstream.

> **This describes a project in development, not a launch announcement.** EduBoost has not been verified as a live school service. No learner recruitment or learner-data access is currently in place for the LEV workstream.

## EduBoost in a minute

EduBoost is a South African learning-support platform in development, with an intended initial focus on Grade 4 Mathematics. The planned experience combines curriculum-linked questions, lessons and practice, a suggested study plan, progress estimates, and an AI-supported tutor.

The project is exploring whether better-targeted practice and clearer progress information can support learners and the adults helping them. That is a hypothesis, not a result from a completed study. We do not yet have evidence that EduBoost improves achievement, works equally well for different groups, or is ready for classroom use.

Educators are being invited to question and shape the work. At this stage, that means discussion and professional input. It does not mean sharing learner records, recruiting pupils, or agreeing to a classroom trial.

## What problem is EduBoost trying to address?

Learners can find some parts of a subject harder than others. In a busy classroom, it can be difficult to tailor practice to each learner and see whether support is helping over time.

EduBoost is exploring three questions:

1. Can curriculum-linked questions give a useful signal about which topic a learner may need to practise?
2. Can that signal help suggest an appropriate next activity, lesson, or study plan?
3. Can educators and researchers determine whether those suggestions are accurate, fair, safe, and useful?

These are questions for educators to challenge. A computer score is not automatically a good measure of understanding, and more practice is not automatically better teaching.

## What does EduBoost aim to do?

If the project is later authorized for a reviewed pilot, the intended learner-support journey is:

1. **Check a skill:** the learner answers questions associated with a curriculum topic.
2. **Estimate a learning need:** the system uses the response pattern and question characteristics to estimate which topics may need more practice.
3. **Suggest a next step:** it can offer related practice, a lesson, or a study-plan activity.
4. **Offer help:** a tutor feature can respond to a learner question using lesson context.
5. **Review progress over time:** the proposed LEV workstream would examine whether the measures and recommendations are trustworthy and whether learners benefit.

This is intended to support educators and learners, not replace teacher judgement, curriculum planning, or relationships with learners.

## How does it work in practice?

### Curriculum-linked questions

The initial Mathematics item bank is tagged to selected CAPS topic references. The 120-row launch bank is quarantined and being rebuilt. Its approval labels were generated by the pipeline and do not establish independent educator approval. It covered only three topic references and is not full CAPS coverage.

### The model estimates; it does not know

Adaptive learner check-ins and mastery views are currently disabled while the question bank and measurement approach are rebuilt and independently reviewed. The code includes a statistical approach called Item Response Theory, but learners should not receive its provisional estimates. In plain language, it considers whether answers are right or wrong together with information about how difficult or informative each question is. It calculates an estimate that may help guide practice.

That estimate is not a report-card grade, a permanent label, or proof that a learner has or has not mastered a topic. Its accuracy depends on the questions and their calibration. The current code has not been shown to make reliable estimates for a representative group of real learners.

### Suggested practice and study plans

Study plans must not use provisional mastery or placement estimates. During the rebuild, any available practice should be fixed and educator-levelled; the current launch study-plan template is quarantined. The first reviewed slice is a pipeline proof for educators to judge, not a finished practice product.

### AI-supported tutoring

The code includes a tutor flow that can use a configured language-model provider to respond to a learner question. It includes safety and privacy-related processing, but an AI response can still be incorrect, incomplete, or unsuitable. Real-world provider operation has not been independently validated for classroom use. Treat the tutor as support that needs adult oversight, not as an authority.

### Human review matters

Educators can help assess whether questions reflect what is taught, whether explanations make sense, whether suggested practice is appropriate, and whether the progress information would be useful. A content approval label or passing software test does not by itself show that a question is pedagogically sound.

## What is LEV?

LEV means **Longitudinal Educational Validation**. "Longitudinal" means looking across time, rather than checking only one session. The workstream is intended to examine whether EduBoost's learning measures and recommendations are dependable, useful, and fair.

The codebase contains analysis methods for measurement accuracy, changes over time, possible differences between learner groups, and whether learning transfers beyond a practice activity. But the LEV traceability service is not connected to the normal learner journey. No real learner cohort, approved study protocol, or field result has been established.

LEV is currently a research and validation workstream being prepared. It is not a live monitoring system, and there are no results showing EduBoost has improved learning.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is EduBoost available for my class now?

There is no verified live school service or authorised live learner use. This is an exploratory discussion document, not an invitation to start using the software with a class.

### Is EduBoost only for Mathematics?

The intended initial focus is a small Grade 4 Mathematics slice. The codebase contains other subject and content work, but that is not the same as approved, complete, classroom-ready coverage.

### Is it aligned to CAPS?

The quarantined launch items were tagged to selected CAPS topic references. That supports only a limited alignment claim, not current availability for learner practice. It does not mean the product covers the full Grade 4 curriculum or that every generated lesson has been independently reviewed by educators.

### Does EduBoost use artificial intelligence?

The tutor code can use a configured AI language-model provider. Diagnostic check-ins and learner mastery views are disabled while the question bank and measurement methods are rebuilt. If re-enabled after review, scoring and progress estimates would use statistical methods and question information. "AI-powered" does not mean the system understands a child as a teacher does or always gives a correct answer.

### Does a diagnostic result tell me what a learner knows?

It is an estimate based on answers to selected questions. Treat it as one possible signal for further discussion or practice, not a definitive judgement about a learner. The estimate needs validation with appropriate learners and educators before it can be relied on.

### Has EduBoost been shown to improve learning?

No. The project has software code and automated tests, but no completed independent field evaluation has established learning impact, fairness, or effectiveness.

### Will EduBoost replace teachers or tutors?

No. Its purpose is to explore digital practice and support. Educator judgement, classroom context, and relationships with learners remain essential.

### Can educators see their learners' records in EduBoost?

A verified educator classroom or roster feature is not established. The teacher-assignment authorization check in the code is a placeholder. Do not assume that a teacher account can safely view assigned learners.

### Are you asking me to recruit learners or share learner data?

No. There is currently no learner recruitment or learner-data access for the LEV workstream. Please do not send names, marks, learner work, contact details, or other identifiable or confidential learner information.

Any future learner study would need a defined and independently reviewed protocol, appropriate school and guardian permissions, clear consent and withdrawal processes, privacy and security safeguards, and the necessary legal and ethics approvals before recruitment or data collection.

### What information might a future evaluation examine?

That has not been finalised. A future protocol might examine how learners respond to curriculum-linked activities over time and whether system estimates match educator judgement or other appropriate evidence. The exact data, measures, retention period, access rules, comparison design, and analysis must be agreed before collection. No learner information should be assumed to be collected for LEV today.

### Is learner information sent to an AI provider?

The tutor code can call an external model provider when configured. Which provider, what information it receives, and what safeguards apply must be settled and reviewed before real learner use. Do not put learner information into a prototype or test environment.

### Is EduBoost compliant with POPIA?

This prospectus does not claim legal compliance. Privacy-related code and controls exist, but legal review, deployment configuration, and operational evidence are still needed. Questions about children's personal information and cross-border provider transfers need advice from qualified South African privacy counsel.

### Does it work offline or in low-connectivity settings?

The codebase contains some offline and low-data work, but a deployed and tested learner experience under limited connectivity has not been established. Do not rely on offline availability today.

### What would you ask educators to contribute?

For now, professional perspective is most useful: which Grade 4 Mathematics difficulties matter in practice; whether selected topics and questions are appropriate; what progress would be meaningful; whether suggested activities would help; what safety and privacy conditions are necessary; and what a responsible future evaluation should show.

A conversation does not commit an educator, school, learner, or family to participate in research or use a product.

### Is there a confirmed time commitment or compensation?

No schedule, formal role, or compensation arrangement is established here. Those details would need to be discussed and agreed before any formal educator activity.

## How to take part in the conversation

If interested, ask the person who shared this prospectus to arrange an exploratory discussion. You can help challenge the problem definition, content choices, progress measures, and assumptions about how schools work.

At this stage, the request is for professional feedback only. There is no request to provide learner data, recruit children, run a classroom trial, or endorse EduBoost.
